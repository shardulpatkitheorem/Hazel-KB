Design intent

The target experience is self-service and fintech-like: a bank finds hazelnetwork.com, picks a plan, clicks through. The BD conversation should be optional, not mandatory. Traditional bank sales cycles run ~2 years; the goal is months.

Open

01

Eligibility Screening

A pre-NDA filter on invited and self-registered banks. Pull public data, score the bank, and green-light or turn away before anything confidential changes hands.

RAFA (UBPR public data)Prelim → DocuSignHOPNDA
Stakeholders3 roles
  • LeadShawn MainBusiness Development — owner
  • SupJoel OlivaresRisk — RAFA composite
  • SupJonathan TateLegal — NDA terms
  1. 01.1

    Bank enters — by invitation or self-registration. Two routes: Vantage selects a bank from the prospecting list and invites it in, or the bank self-registers through a public inquiry form — entering its FDIC number, website, and contact name/title/phone, then submitting interest. The applicant gets a thank-you and a confirmation email; Vantage is notified, runs RAFA, and if it passes, sends an invitation. Either way the entry feeds the same RAFA screening.

    • No auto-fill search bar for MVP-1. The bank types its own details rather than picking from a name lookup — a safeguard against selecting the wrong similarly-named institution and against bot-submitted fake requests.
    Shawn Main · BDpublic inquiry form
  2. 01.2

    RAFA report pulled from public UBPR data — a CAMELS-style composite computed from the bank's own public filings, run in the background on the FDIC number the bank entered. This is an internal, back-office screening step; for the first 10–15 banks it may be run manually. Auto-prefill from a name/FDIC search is deferred for MVP (see 01.1).

    RAFA APIbanks only — no credit unions yet

Does the bank meet pre-NDA criteria?

Yes

RAFA composite 1 or 2 → continue.

Conditional

No

  1. 01.3

    Send and execute the NDA through Prelim. NDA signing runs on Prelim, which handles the document workflow and e-signature, rather than as an ad-hoc document.

    Prelim → DocuSign
  2. 01.4

    Invitation email and account registration. On approval the invitation email goes out via SendGrid. The bank then registers an account — the login for HOP, the Hazel dashboard. The authentication method (MFA vs OAuth) is still open.

    HOPSendGridauth: MFA / OAuth TBD
Decided

Entry now has two routes: Vantage invitation and a self-service public inquiry form — a bank can register on its own, not only by invitation. NDA signing runs through Prelim. The invitation email is sent via SendGrid. There is no auto-fill search bar for MVP-1 (security). Once admitted, the bank registers an account for HOP, the Hazel dashboard. (Resolves the earlier Q1 on entry route and Q6 on email provider.)

Scale

Rejection is an edge case — expected under 1%, not 10–20%. Of ~4,298 banks, roughly 15 sit on the watch list. Undercapitalised banks are the clear decline.

What the bank sees

Not the raw 1–5 score — banks would try to reconcile it against their real regulator rating. Show a traffic light or star rating; detail only through a conversation. There is no disclosure obligation when turning a bank away, unlike a consumer credit denial.

Open

With the self-service form now in scope, anti-abuse is active work: verify the email is a bank domain, MFA, sit behind Cloudflare, restrict countries at launch.

Intake & Due Diligence

Collect documents, score the bank properly, and check for anything that forces a human review. Runs in both directions — the bank will also want to diligence Vantage/Hazel.

  1. 02.1

    Receive signed NDA from the bank.

  2. 02.2

    Bank added to Coverbase as a new vendor; questionnaire issued. Under 30 questions, mostly yes/no.

    The applicant never sees Coverbase. The Hazel portal is the single front end; Coverbase runs headless behind an API while back-office staff work inside it. Confirmed in build: the member portal never exposes the internal Coverbase scoring — the bank sees only a general status.

  3. 02.3

    Bank uploads the two required documents — acceptance gates due diligence. The due-diligence form stays locked until both are uploaded and accepted; a rejected document loops back for re-upload.

    Wolfsberg CBDDQ — or mini-Wolfsberg PDFBSA / AML / OFAC policy
    • Wolfsberg is conditional. Not all banks hold a Wolfsberg document, even FDIC ones. If the bank has it, it uploads and continues; if not, Vantage supplies a mini-Wolfsberg — a shortened PDF of only the most relevant questions (not the full 16–19 pages, and not branded Wolfsberg) that the bank fills, signs via DocuSign, and returns, stored alongside the NDA and BSA policy. Leaning to a DocuSign PDF rather than an in-page form; whether the answers are also captured as structured data depends on whether any answer drives downstream configuration.
    • One combined flow. The due-diligence intake is a single scrolling form with header sections and one Submit at the bottom — no per-section save buttons. Answers are cached client-side and written to the backend in one go; the overview separately tracks completion across the major steps (NDA, documents, due diligence, risk, review).
    • Coverbase AI can auto-answer the questionnaire from the uploaded documents — the bank then reviews each drafted answer and either confirms, marks "unable to confirm," or corrects it. Risk questions stay locked until the due-diligence section is complete.
    • Upload triggers the Coverbase intake. Uploading the two documents prompts Vantage to create the Coverbase intake form. The due-diligence questions are surfaced in the Hazel experience (some dynamic, some static) and pushed back to Coverbase via API for the residual-risk assessment; the 32 inherent-risk questions are compared against Vantage's controls to produce the risk and residual-risk PDF.
    • All intake data — the uploaded documents and the answers collected in the Hazel experience — lands in Databricks first, then pushes out to Coverbase (and Prelim); the documents are held in both. See the Decided note below for the caveat on what stays Coverbase-only.
    • If the applicant is not a regulated bank, deeper diligence is required — and no intake form exists for that case yet.
  4. 02.4

    Reverse due diligence — the bank vets Vantage/Hazel through the Vanta trust center: request access, get approved, download the document set.

    • The Trust Center is a Vantage skin over Vanta, packaging Vantage + Custodia + Infinant due-diligence data (SOC reports, financials, risk assessments) as the Hazel Network due-diligence package.
    • NDA carries over: the NDA signed in intake (Step 1) auto-satisfies the Vanta NDA requirement, so the bank does not sign again — Vanta still needs it on file to prove NDA-before-disclosure to examiners.
    • Capture anything they ask for that isn't already covered, and publish the answer for every future bank. No one-offs.
    • A full RFI/RFP demand is handled as a separate side process, not part of onboarding.
    Vanta · trust.vantage.bank

    Consent order check. Manual today — there is no central database, so each regulator's site must be checked individually.

    BSA · Joel Castanedacandidate: American Bankers enforcement DB
  6. 02.6

    Automated initial risk score run in Coverbase. Risk domains scored by percentage; final rating on a 0–4 scale (a “2” is roughly 50% risk).

Philosophy

These are FDIC-insured banks, so Hazel does not re-regulate them — it verifies that they are regulated and asks a narrow set of questions. Documents requested are ones banks already hold.

Open

The numeric thresholds that separate auto-approve, manual review and auto-reject are not yet defined in Coverbase — the single most-cited gap in the whole workflow.

Open

Rather than reject borderline banks, run the assessment against the Hazel rule book and return a list of deficiencies to fix. This creates a likely new state: approved, but blocked from transacting until conditions clear. The rule book does not exist yet.

Decided

Review of the Coverbase API confirms everything needed here can be driven through it. Storage: all intake data — uploaded documents and the answers collected in the Hazel experience — lands in Databricks first, then pushes to Coverbase (and Prelim); the documents live in both. Coverbase's own residual-risk assessment and internal scoring stay in Coverbase. Caveat: this is Theorem's stated architecture and revises the earlier "no Q&A in Databricks" position — Vantage / Shawn still to lock the final data-retention line.

03

Approval

Either the system approves automatically and the committee is simply told, or the case goes to the committee to decide.

Banking Innovation Risk Committee
Stakeholders3 roles
  • DecBanking Innovation Risk CtteeAcknowledges or decides
  • LeadShawn MainBusiness Development — presents
  • SupJoel OlivaresRisk — approval package
Gate 3

Are the auto-approval criteria met?

Yes

In-parameter bank — composite 1–2, clear Wolfsberg, no exceptions → auto-approved. The committee acknowledges at its next meeting.

No

On the fence — composite 3 or 4 — or an exception case → the committee decides.

  1. 03.1

    Committee acknowledgement or decision. Expected to be light-touch at scale: a weekly report of banks onboarded, their scores and any exceptions — not a bank-by-bank gate for hundreds of applicants.

Prerequisite

The auto-approval criteria must be designed, documented and codified as governance policy before this step can run as intended. Board approval is not expected for Basic member banks — the bank opens an account at Vantage, not the reverse. Regulation F guidance is being confirmed.

Working view

“On the fence” should largely disappear. Either a bank meets hard rules, or it is admitted with rule-book conditions or capital requirements attached. Practical outcomes: approve · approve-with-conditions · rare reject.

04

Setup & Contracting

Approval unlocks two tracks that run at the same time: build the empty tenant, and get the paperwork signed. Accounts cannot be created until both land.

Infinant / InterlacePrelim → DocuSign
Stakeholders6 roles
  • LeadDiana PlataBaaS — setup and configuration
  • LeadJonathan TateLegal — partner package
  • SMEMarie AlonzoOps — account opening, signers
  • ExtInfinantProvisions the tenant
  • ExtPrelim (Norma)Document and signature workflow
  • BankMember bankSigner roster, product election, signing
  1. 04.1

    Collect setup information and the Infinant configuration workbook.

    • Authorised signer roster · operating account list and intended use · operations and escalation contacts
    • All rails offered unless the bank opts out
    • Participate-referred, or not

    May need little bank input — a prior tenant's config was simply copied. But the bank must be asked which products it actually wants, so cloning doesn't open accounts it then has to disclose and manage.

Track A — build the shell

Infinant creates the tenant. Manual today, ~2 weeks, no self-serve API.

  1. Submit the configuration workbook to Infinant.
  2. Infinant clones the last tenant and provisions a new one.
  3. Account numbers are assigned — required before the signature card and wire agreement can be sent.

Track B — get it signed

Documents go out in batches, some before provisioning and some after.

  1. Account-opening package — ~5 PDFs. Manual PDF delivery for pilot banks; future state is a Prelim or custom workflow.
  2. Partner / legal package — Hazel membership agreement linking to an MSA and correspondent agreement, plus a security procedures document. Non-negotiable for Basic.
  3. Signer identity from the resolution doc, operating agreement or bylaws.
Merge point. Signatures gate account creation. Working sequence (being simulated): approval → tenant shell created → bank completes account setup information → account number prepared and provided → agreements presented and signed via DocuSign (not yet integrated) → accepted → the two accounts are created inside the shell.
Two document sets

Steps 1–2 collect documents that admit the bank to the network. Step 4 collects a separate set that opens the accounts — signature cards, wire agreements, beneficial ownership, disclaimers.

Open

Where does the create-account API call fire from? Prelim calling Interlace directly, or Prelim returning data to the Hazel app which then makes the call. Leaning toward the latter so status and documents are captured in Databricks and Hazel acts as middleware. The Prelim↔Interlace integration does not exist yet.

Decided

No account-number-entry field. A shortcut letting a bank type an existing account number was considered and dropped: a bank in initial intake has no Hazel account yet, so it must create one. There is no standalone "Participate account" to reuse either.

Open

Once the bank has console access it can open additional operating accounts on its own, with no documents generated. Those sit on Vantage's balance sheet. Direction: have the original agreements permit additional accounts inheriting the same beneficial ownership. Accounts the bank opens for its own customers are not Vantage's concern — Hazel is the ledgering platform there.

Limits

Two layers. Vantage/Hazel sets an overall program limit from the bank's risk profile; account-level limits are agreed with the bank beneath it. The bank then sets its own limits within its products.

Open

The two-week Infinant lead time is the bottleneck — the request must be raised far earlier, potentially before due diligence completes. Provisioning scope and the ownership split between Infinant and Vantage still need confirming; the DDA-creation fix is pending on Infinant.

Open

Compliance model. In Basic the bank outsources monitoring to Vantage, which uses Sardine as its own backstop; the bank has no Sardine contract. Two alternatives were discussed — bank dispositions natively in Sardine, or brings its own provider — both with contract and pricing consequences. Fraud alert volume will drive staffing.

Gate 4

Is the bank still meeting the rule book?

Yes

Continue in production; periodic reporting only.

Breach

Cure period and warnings, potentially penalties or fines. Terms to be defined in the rule book with Legal.

Severe

Immediate kill switch — stop the bank's ability to transact.

Open

Liability. If Hazel recommends a fix, the bank ignores it, Hazel admits them anyway and something goes wrong — where does liability sit? Direction: anchor everything in the rule book and operating rules, modelled on how established payment networks handle it.

Network view

Hazel behaves as a network operator and needs its own sight of the daily Interlace side-core to main-core reconciliation — delays, calculation errors, disputes — because the network becomes the final authority to complain to. Interlace transactions, Custodia mint/burn and chain-analysis data all stream into Databricks and surface as drillable dashboards: one story per bank, one for the network.

Open items blocking the flow

  1. Auto-approval criteria — must be designed, documented and codified as policy before Step 3 runs as intended.
  2. Coverbase score thresholds — the numbers separating auto-approve, review and reject.
  3. The Hazel rule book — risk and transaction controls, conditions, penalties, cure periods, kill switch. Paused, needs restarting with Legal.
  4. Sardine / compliance-as-a-service model — who monitors, under which contract, at what staffing level.
  5. Infinant scope and lead time — the two-week provisioning window, the DDA fix, and exactly what data can be streamed out.
  6. Contracting engine — reuse Prelim or rebuild in-app, and where the create-account call fires from.
  7. Basic product list — which products exist, which the bank may self-open, and how product type maps to account configuration.
  8. Self-provisioned accounts — documentation and compliance for accounts the bank opens itself.
  9. Non-bank intake — credit unions, trust charters and foreign banks need their own questionnaires and data sources.
  10. Consent order automation — currently a manual regulator-by-regulator lookup.
  11. Support and ticketing model — channels, hours, SLAs, and whether ticketing is built inside Databricks.
  12. Stakeholder and integration mapping — which function and which system at every step. Requested, not yet done.
  13. UBPR lookup field — Theorem Labs to determine which identifier drives the RAFA UBPR search (FDIC field or another).
  14. Legal package — membership agreement, MSA, correspondent agreement and security procedures, reconciled against the embedded-bank versions before going online.
